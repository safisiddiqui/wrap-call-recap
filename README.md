# Wrap

Turn your notes from a customer call into two things you can actually use: an internal account note, and a follow-up email you can send.

[Live demo](PASTE-YOUR-LIVE-LINK-HERE)

## Why

Every CSM writes the same two things after every call. One for the record, so the account isn't a mystery in six weeks. One for the customer, so everyone agrees on what happened. It's not hard work, but it's constant, and it always lands when your attention is worst.

Plenty of software records calls and writes summaries. This isn't that. This starts from what's in your head thirty seconds after the call, including the part you'd never say out loud on it.

## What it does

Fill in what happened: who was on the call, what you covered, what got decided, anything you're worried about, and next steps.

You get back:

- **An internal note.** Structured, skimmable, with risks flagged separately.
- **A customer email.** Same call, written for the person on the other side.

Copy either one with a button.

## Two decisions worth naming

**Risks stay internal.** "Their champion is changing roles" belongs in your account record, not in the recap email. The tool splits them, so the worry shows up in the internal note and simply doesn't exist in the customer version.

**Every next step has an owner.** A follow-up email with unowned action items is a list nobody does. The tool asks for the task, the person, and the date, and says so out loud when the owner is missing.

Those aren't technical choices. They come from having sent a lot of these emails and noticing which ones got a reply.

## How it works

One HTML file. No build step, no dependencies, no framework.

Nothing you type leaves your browser. There's no server, no account, and no data stored anywhere.

## Running it

Open `index.html` in a browser. That's the whole setup.

## Limitations

It formats your notes. It doesn't improve them. Vague input produces a confident-looking recap built on vagueness, which is worse than obviously rough notes because you're less likely to catch it before sending. This speeds up the writing, not the thinking.
